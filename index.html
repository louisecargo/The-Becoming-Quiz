Section 02

Breathwork Practice

Less than ten minutes. No equipment. Just your breath and a quiet space. Return to this whenever you need to slow the spiral and come back to yourself.

The Returning Breath 8–10 minutes

Find a comfortable position. Close your eyes or soften your gaze. Take a moment to arrive before you begin.

1

Ground (2 minutes)

Begin with your natural breath. Don't change anything yet. Just notice where you feel it. You're not trying to relax. You're just arriving.

2

Slow (3 minutes)

Inhale for a count of four. Hold gently for two. Exhale slowly for six. Let the exhale be longer than the inhale.

3

Ask (2 minutes)

Bring your attention to the centre of your chest. Ask quietly: what is true for me right now? Don't analyse. Just create the space for something to surface.

4

Return (2 minutes)

Let your breath return to its natural rhythm. On the last exhale, let it go completely. Feel the natural pause before the next inhale arrives.

When to use this

Before your morning check-in. Before a difficult conversation. When the fog descends. When you've been attending to everyone else and lost track of yourself.

Section 04

Hormonal Refocus

Perimenopause doesn't move in a straight line. Some weeks are clearer. Some are harder. Maybe you are deep in the fog, in a longer than usual cycle, or in a never-ending PMS depression. This section gives you something to reach for depending on where you are — so you're not starting from scratch on the days when you have the least to give. This will help you refocus when everything feels hard.

A note on this section

This is not medical advice. It's practical support for the emotional reality of this season. Use what helps. Leave what doesn't. If you're struggling in a way that feels bigger than this, please reach out to a healthcare professional.

What's Actually Working 10–15 minutes · pen and paper

A practice for the days when everything feels like it's falling apart. Not a gratitude list — something more honest than that. A way of helping your attention find what's solid, even when everything else feels uncertain.

1Name one relationship in your life that feels safe and real right now.
2Name one thing your body did for you today — something small it did without you having to ask.
3Name one thing you handled this week that you didn't think you could.
4Name one place where you feel most like yourself. What is it about that place?
5Name one thing you are still doing in this season that matters to you.
6Name one moment in the last week when you felt — even briefly — like yourself.
7Look at your answers. What do they tell you about what's actually solid in your life right now?
When to use this

Particularly useful in the lead up to your period when the emotional weight feels heaviest. The hormonal drop can make the fog feel permanent — this practice is a gentle reminder that it isn't.

For the Pre-Period Drop

When the depression and heaviness arrive in the lead up to your period, here's what to reach for:

1Pull a card — let something land without having to figure anything out
2Do the Returning Breath — even just steps 1 and 2 if that's all you have
3Work through What's Actually Working — slowly, without pressure
4Remind yourself: this is hormonal. It will shift. You are not your worst days.
The most important thing

The fog feeling permanent is a symptom of where you are in your cycle — not a truth about your life. The clarity will return. It always does.
